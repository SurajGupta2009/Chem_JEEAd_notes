## Oxidation states

O.S. of S in H₂SO₅, and why the naive answer is wrong?::+6. It has a peroxide O–O (two O at −1); "all O = −2" gives an impossible +8 (§5)

O.S. of Cr in CrO₅?::+6: one oxo O (−2) plus four peroxide O (−1) (§5)

The two S atoms in Na₂S₂O₃ carry which O.S.?::−2 (terminal S) and +6 (central S); average +2 (§5)

Average O.S. of S in S₄O₆²⁻, and the individual values?::+2.5 on average; the two bridging S are 0 and the two end S are +5 (§5)

The two N in NH₄NO₃, and their average?::−3 and +5; average +1 (§5)

O.S. of Fe in the brown-ring complex [Fe(H₂O)₅NO]²⁺ (JEE answer)?::+1, with NO present as NO⁺ (§5)

O.S. of Fe in sodium nitroprusside Na₂[Fe(CN)₅NO]?::+2 (NO counted as NO⁺) (§5)

O.S. of O in KO₂, O₂F₂ and OF₂?::−½, +1, +2 (§4, §5)

NCl₃: O.S. of N and Cl, and the evidence?::N −3, Cl +1. Hydrolysis gives NH₃ + HOCl with no O.S. change (§4)

Why is H₄P₂O₆ P +4 while H₄P₂O₇ is P +5?::H₄P₂O₆ (hypophosphoric acid) has a P–P bond, which contributes 0 (§5)

O.S. of C in ethanol and in acetic acid (each carbon)?::Ethanol −3, −1; acetic acid −3, +3 (§5)

Give a compound with a metal in a negative O.S.::Na[Co(CO)₄] (Co −1) or Na₂[Fe(CO)₄] (Fe −2) (§6)

In HOF (NCERT Ex. 7.4), what is the O.S. of oxygen?::0 (H +1, F −1) (§21)

## Types and roles

Which species cannot disproportionate: ClO⁻, ClO₂⁻, ClO₃⁻, ClO₄⁻?::ClO₄⁻, because Cl is already at its highest O.S. (+7) (§7d)

Why doesn't F₂ disproportionate in alkali?::F has no positive O.S.; 2F₂ + 2OH⁻ → 2F⁻ + OF₂ + H₂O is simple reduction of F (§7d)

Products of Cl₂ with cold dilute vs hot concentrated NaOH?::Cold: Cl⁻ + ClO⁻. Hot: Cl⁻ + ClO₃⁻ (5 : 1) (§7d)

Products of P₄ with NaOH solution?::PH₃ + H₂PO₂⁻ (P 0 → −3 and +1) (§7d)

Why does a permanganate titration with too little acid go brown?::MnO₄⁻ stops at MnO₂, and Mn²⁺ + MnO₄⁻ comproportionate to MnO₂ (§7e, §12a)

Why must bleach never be mixed with acid?::Cl⁻ + ClO⁻ + 2H⁺ → Cl₂ + H₂O (comproportionation) releases chlorine gas (§7e)

Why can SO₂ and H₂O₂ act as oxidant and reductant, but HNO₃ only as oxidant?::S (+4) and O (−1) are at intermediate O.S.; N in HNO₃ is at its maximum, +5 (§8)

Fe + excess I₂ gives which iodide, and why?::FeI₂ only. I₂ (0.54 V) cannot oxidise Fe²⁺ to Fe³⁺ (0.77 V) (§8)

Why does CuI₂ not exist?::Cu²⁺ oxidises I⁻: 2Cu²⁺ + 4I⁻ → 2CuI + I₂ (§2, §8)

Conc. H₂SO₄ with a chloride vs a bromide?::Chloride gives only HCl; bromide is oxidised to Br₂ (+ SO₂) (§8)

Is Fe³⁺ + SCN⁻ → [Fe(SCN)]²⁺ a redox reaction?::No: complexation, no O.S. change (§19, §20)

## Balancing and n-factor

MnO₄⁻ : I⁻ mole ratio in acid, in base (→ I₂), and with excess alkaline MnO₄⁻ (→ IO₃⁻)?::1 : 5, 1 : 3, 2 : 1 (§10)

In basic-medium ion–electron balancing, what do you do with H⁺?::Add as many OH⁻ as there are H⁺ to both sides, then combine H⁺ + OH⁻ into H₂O (§10)

n-factor of KMnO₄ in acid / neutral / strongly alkaline medium?::5 / 3 / 1 (§11)

n-factor of FeC₂O₄ with acidified KMnO₄?::3: Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ (1) plus C₂O₄²⁻ → 2CO₂ (2) (§11)

n-factor of Na₂S₂O₃ with I₂ vs with Cl₂?::1 (→ S₄O₆²⁻) vs 8 (→ SO₄²⁻) (§11)

n-factor of H₃PO₃ as an acid?::2: it is dibasic, because one H is bonded to P (§11)

## Titrations

Why is KMnO₄ titrated in dilute H₂SO₄, not HCl?::Cl⁻ is oxidised to Cl₂, consuming extra KMnO₄ (high titre) (§12a)

Why warm oxalate before titrating with KMnO₄, and what does overheating do?::The reaction is slow until Mn²⁺ autocatalyses it. Overheating decomposes oxalic acid, giving a low titre (§12a)

Role of HgCl₂ in the SnCl₂ reduction of iron ore?::It removes excess Sn²⁺ as white Hg₂Cl₂ (§12a)

Indicator and colour change for Fe²⁺ vs K₂Cr₂O₇?::Diphenylamine: green → violet-blue (§12b)

Why can K₂Cr₂O₇ be used in HCl but KMnO₄ cannot?::E°(Cr₂O₇²⁻/Cr³⁺) 1.33 V < E°(Cl₂/Cl⁻) 1.36 V < E°(MnO₄⁻/Mn²⁺) 1.51 V (§12b)

Iodometry vs iodimetry: which end point shows blue appearing?::Iodimetry (titrating a reductant with I₂). In iodometry the blue disappears (§12c)

Cu²⁺ : S₂O₃²⁻ ratio in iodometric copper estimation?::1 : 1 (each Cu²⁺ gains only one electron) (§12c)

Maximum "available chlorine" in pure CaOCl₂?::About 55.8 % (70.9 / 127) (§12c)

## Cells and potentials

Three jobs of a salt bridge?::Completes the circuit; keeps each half-cell neutral; prevents mixing (also minimises junction potential) (§14)

Why not a KCl salt bridge with a silver half-cell?::AgCl precipitates; use KNO₃ or NH₄NO₃ (§14)

Why is Li the strongest reductant in water despite its high ionisation enthalpy?::The very large hydration enthalpy of the small Li⁺ ion (§15)

Is Ag + Fe³⁺ → Ag⁺ + Fe²⁺ feasible?::No: E°cell = 0.77 − 0.80 = −0.03 V (§16)

Which metals give H₂ with very dilute HNO₃?::Mg and Mn (§16)

Order of displacement for Al, Cu, Fe, Mg, Zn?::Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Cu (§16)

## Advanced

Latimer rule for disproportionation?::A species disproportionates if E° of the link on its right > E° of the link on its left (§17)

Why is Cl₂ stable in acid but disproportionated in base?::Acid: 1.36 < 1.63 (right < left). Base: 1.36 > 0.42 (§17)

E°(Fe³⁺/Fe) from Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ (0.77) and Fe²⁺/Fe (−0.44)?::(0.77 − 0.88)/3 = −0.037 V; combine through ΔG°, never a plain sum (§17)

K for 2Cu⁺ ⇌ Cu²⁺ + Cu at 298 K?::E° = 0.368 V, so log K = 6.23 and K ≈ 1.7 × 10⁶ (§20)

Order of oxidising power: perchlorate, perbromate, periodate?::Perbromate > periodate > perchlorate (middle-row anomaly) (§18)

The disproportionating elements in NCERT Ex. 7.24 are {{P, S, Cl}} (non-metals) and {{Cu, Mn, In}} (metals) (§21)
